# Google Play policy and technical requirements research: Plants vs Animals

- **App:** com.oeogames.plantsvsanimals. Unity 6000.3, targetSdk 36, minSdk 25. Target audience includes children (mixed audience).
- **Research date / access date for every URL:** 2026-10-19
- **Method and limits:**
  - Pages were read through a fetch tool that returns quotes of 125 characters or less. Long policy sentences therefore appear below as consecutive fragments joined with "+" or "...".
  - Direct `curl` to support.google.com was blocked by the sandbox proxy, so no full raw HTML was saved.
  - Quotes are verbatim as returned by the fetch tool. Anything marked "from memory, unverified" was not confirmed on a page today.

**Labels used in this document:**
- **FACT:** quoted from an official page.
- **ESTIMATE:** a judgement about likelihood or size.
- **ASSUMPTION/INTERPRETATION:** my reading of the policy. It is not Google's wording.

## 1. Families Policy: requirements for apps whose audience includes children

Primary source: Google Play Families Policies, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en (accessed 2026-10-19).

### Who it applies to
- **FACT** (Manage target audience, answer/9867159): "Any apps that include children in their target audience must comply with Google Play's Families Policy Requirements."
- **FACT:** Mixed-audience selection is worded "Your app is designed for everyone, **including children**."

### Ads and monetization (answer/9893335)
- **FACT:** "If your app displays ads to children or to users of unknown age, you must:"
  - "Only use Google Play Families Self-Certified Ads SDKs to display ads to those users;"
  - "Ensure ads displayed to those users do not involve interest-based advertising" + "or remarketing (advertising targeted at individual users based on previous interaction with an app or website);"
- **FACT:** The policy lists these as not allowed:
  - "Disruptive monetization and advertising, including monetization and advertising that take up the entire screen" [fragment; the full sentence continues past the quote limit]
  - "Monetization and advertising that interfere with normal app use or game play, including rewarded or opt-in ads" ... "that are not closeable after 5 seconds."
  - "Deceptive ads that force the user to click-through by using a dismiss button to trigger another ad"
  - "Interstitial monetization and advertising displayed immediately upon app launch."
  - "Multiple ad placements on a page"
  - "Monetization and advertising that are not clearly distinguishable from your app content"
  - "Use of shocking or emotionally manipulative tactics to encourage ads viewing or in-app purchases."
  - "Not providing a distinction between the use of virtual game coins versus real-life money to make in-app purchases."
- **FACT:** "Google Play reserves the right to reject, remove or suspend apps for overly aggressive commercial tactics."
- **FACT** (mixed audience, policy summary): "If your app is for a mixed audience, you must include a neutral age screen to ensure children only see non-personalized ads."
- **FACT:** In the body text the requirement reads "you must implement age screening measures, such as a neutral age screen".

**Rewarded ads are allowed.** No sentence prohibits them. The policy explicitly regulates "rewarded or opt-in ads", which must be closeable after 5 seconds when they interfere with game play.
- **INTERPRETATION:** The "closeable after 5 seconds" wording is ambiguous for a full rewarded video, where the reward is earned by watching the whole ad. A plausible reading is that the ad must be closable after 5 s, and the reward may be withheld if the player closes it early. This is not confirmed by Google wording. I found no Google page that explains this specifically for rewarded ads in Families apps. Flag it as uncertain.

### Families Self-Certified Ads SDK list: is AdMob on it?
- **FACT** (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12955712?hl=en, "Participate in the Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Program"): the table row reads "Google AdMob | com.google.android.gms:play-services-ads | 19.0.0 or later". The same table also lists Unity Ads (4.0.1 or later), ironSource (7.2.1 or later), and Google Ad Manager IMA/PAL.
- **FACT:** The program is "currently not accepting new applicants" (answer/12955712 and answer/9900633).

### Self-Certified Ads SDK policy (obligations on SDKs)
Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12918983?hl=en
- **FACT:** "You must allow developers, on a per-request or per-app basis, to request child-directed treatment for ad serving."
- **FACT:** "You must not transmit AAID, SIM Serial, Build Serial, BSSID, MAC, SSID, IMEI, and/or IMSI" ... "from children or users of unknown age."

### AdMob's own Families guidance
Source: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6223431?hl=en
- **FACT:** For mixed-audience apps, developers must "include code that calls the `setTagForChildDirectedTreatment()`method". The same page also says `max_ad_content_rating` should be set to `G` "for every ad request" in the mediation/child case.
- **FACT:** For mediation, AdMob says it "will automatically block any ad sources that haven't been self-certified by Google Play".
- **FACT:** For mixed audiences, AdMob advises "Android GMA SDK 20.6.0 or later" so that the ad ID is not sent for child-directed requests (paraphrase from the fetch tool; not quoted verbatim).
- **FACT** (https://developers.google.com/admob/unity/targeting): "The `TagForChildDirectedTreatment` property is deprecated." Also "The age treatment setting replaces the deprecated" TFCD/TFUA, and you use `AgeRestrictedTreatment.Child`.
- **FACT** (https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6219315?hl=en): with child treatment, "The Android Advertising Identifier (AAID) and the iOS Identifier for Advertisers (IDFA) are not transmitted."; "Personalized ads and remarketing are disabled."; "Requests to third-party ad vendors, such as ad measurement pixels and third-party ad servers, are disabled."

### Data collection restrictions
Sources: answer/9893335 and "Data practices in Families apps", https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11043825?hl=en
- **FACT (mixed audience):** "Apps that target both children and older audiences must not transmit AAID, SIM Serial, Build Serial, BSSID," + "MAC, SSID, IMEI, and/or IMSI from children or users of unknown age."
- **FACT:** "Device phone number must not be requested from TelephonyManager of the Android API."
- **FACT (location; the stated rule is for solely child-directed apps):** "Apps that solely target children may not request location permission, or collect, use, and transmit precise location."
- **FACT (AD_ID; the stated rule is for solely child-directed apps):** "Apps solely targeted to children should not request AD_ID permission when targeting Android API 33 or higher."
- **FACT:** "For analytics, developers are encouraged to use app set ID."

### General SDK rule (exact wording)
- **FACT** (answer/11043825):
  - Mixed audience: "Apps that target both children and older audiences must not implement APIs or SDKs that are not approved" + " for use in child-directed services unless they are used behind a neutral age screen"
  - Solely child-directed apps: "should only use SDKs which are approved for use in child-directed services."
- **FACT** (answer/11043825, sign-in): "Apps that target both children and older audiences must not require users to sign-in or access app content" + " through an API or SDK that is not approved for use in child-directed services"
- **FACT** (answer/9893335): "Apps that solely target children must not contain any APIs or SDKs that are not approved for use" + "in primarily child-directed services."
- **FACT:** "You must ensure that your app properly implements any APIs and SDKs."
- **FACT / UNCERTAIN:** I found no official public list of "SDKs approved for use in child-directed services" other than the ads-SDK list. Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics were not named on any Families page I fetched. The Firebase privacy page (https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy) and the Firebase data disclosure page do not mention children or COPPA.

### Upcoming change
- **FACT** (Preview page, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17122218): the July 2026 update expands the Families requirements to "prohibit developers of anonymous chat apps from targeting children", "effective August 26, 2026".

### Implications for Plants vs Animals (INTERPRETATION)
- AdMob (play-services-ads 19.0.0 or later) is on the self-certified list. Rewarded ads that the player chooses to watch are permitted.
- Use `AgeRestrictedTreatment.Child` (TFAT) on every request, or the still-functional deprecated TFCD, with max rating G. This gives no AAID, no personalisation, and no third-party pixels. Child treatment on every request is the most conservative setup and means a neutral age screen is not needed for ads.
- Do not show interstitials at launch. Use one ad placement per screen. Make sure the ad close button becomes available within 5 s, and confirm the actual close behaviour of the AdMob rewarded format on a test device.
- Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics: with AD_ID removed, the AAID is zeroed (FACT, answer/11043825: "AAID will automatically be disabled (set to a string of zeros)"). Their status as "approved for use in child-directed services" is not documented on any page I could fetch. This is the main residual uncertainty for 1.2.0.
  - **ESTIMATE:** risk is moderate-to-low with ad ID collection off, but not zero.
  - Option: put Analytics behind child-safe defaults (consent mode / disable ad personalization) and keep Crashlytics only. You could also consider app set ID.
- Do not ask for location permission. Do not read the phone number or hardware IDs.
- Avoid "watch an ad or lose" pressure screens. They would breach "shocking or emotionally manipulative tactics".

## 2. Play Games Services (PGS v2): leaderboards, achievements, friends, sign-in

### What Google says about PGS and Families / child users
- **FACT:** The Families Policies page (answer/9893335) does not mention "Google Play Games" or "leaderboards". The "Data practices in Families apps" page (answer/11043825) does not mention Play Games Services.
- **FACT:** PGS Terms (https://developer.android.com/games/pgs/terms, last modified "June 26th, 2020") contain no mention of children, COPPA, or Families.
- **FACT:** The PGS quality checklist (https://developer.android.com/games/pgs/quality) contains no children or Families content. It lists "1.1 (Required): Authenticate players with Google Play Games Services."

**Consumer-side statements about supervised child accounts:**
- **FACT** (https://support.google.com/families/answer/7106960?hl=en): children "can create a Play Games profile to represent their gaming identity"; "To create the profile, a parent must give approval."; "some game features dependent on Play Games profiles may not work as expected."
- **FACT** (https://support.google.com/families/answer/9468477): "Not all games support automatic sign-in with a Play Games profile for supervised Google Accounts." Also: "Their profile may have other restrictions, such as the ability to add in-game friends."
- **FACT** (https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2954594?hl=en): "Supervised account profiles can only use auto-generated gamer names."
- **FACT** (https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/3129346?hl=en): "Play Games Profiles for supervised Google Accounts don't have access to the Play Games followers feature."

### Data safety implications of PGS
Source: https://developer.android.com/games/pgs/data-collection
- **FACT:** Collected by default: "Gamer Identity (Gamertag, avatar)". It also collects analytics and diagnostics data "to improve the stability of our SDKs and make product improvements."
- **FACT:** Collected depending on usage: "Unlocked achievements, Games scores, Engagement and spend statistics, Social connections (Friend connections, requests, nicknames for friends)". Saved Games collects "Saved game progress, Saved game screenshot".
- **FACT:** "Play Games Services encrypts the data in transit using HTTPS."
- **FACT:** "Users can delete their account and all associated games data collected by Google through their Play Games profile or Google MyAccount."
- **FACT:** You are "solely responsible for deciding how to respond to Google Play's Data safety section form."

### Age gate / neutral age screen for social features or UGC
- **FACT:** Mixed-audience apps may use non-approved SDKs only "behind a neutral age screen" (answer/11043825).
- **FACT** (answer/9867159): "A neutral age screen is a mechanism to verify a user's age in a way that doesn't encourage them to falsify their age." It gives "presetting the birth date to the required age" as an example of incorrect setup.
- **FACT:** The Families "Social Apps & Features" section covers features that "share freeform content". The section is quoted in Q3.

### Implications for Plants vs Animals (INTERPRETATION)
- No official page says PGS is prohibited in Families apps. No official page says PGS is "approved for use in child-directed services" either. Google's own consumer docs show that PGS works for supervised child accounts with parental approval, auto-generated names for supervised accounts, and restricted friends and followers. That implies Google anticipates child use. It is still not a developer-policy approval.
  - **ESTIMATE:** using PGS for leaderboards and achievements is probably acceptable, but this is unverified.
- Your earlier decision to remove PGS from 1.2.0 out of caution is defensible.
- The lowest-risk path if PGS is re-added:
  - (a) never require PGS sign-in to play (the sign-in rule above);
  - (b) use achievements first, since they involve no shared content;
  - (c) for leaderboards, consider putting them behind a neutral age screen, or accept the residual risk.
- Leaderboards display other players' Play Games gamer names and avatars. A gamer name is user-chosen text for adult accounts.
  - **INTERPRETATION:** Under Google's definition a leaderboard is probably not a "social feature", because it does not "share freeform content or communicate with large groups". This is uncertain, and a reviewer could see it differently.
- Offline, local-only leaderboards (personal best per level) and local achievements carry no Families risk and need no SDK. This fits the "semi-passive" goal best.
- If PGS is added, the Data safety form must reflect gamer identity, game progress and achievements, and possibly friends. Saved Games (cloud save) adds saved-game data.

## 3. User-generated content and social features in Families apps (relevant to ghost 1v1)

Source: answer/9893335, "Social Apps & Features".
- **FACT:** "If your apps allows users to share or exchange information, you must accurately disclose these features" + "in the content rating questionnaire on the Play Console."
- **FACT:** "A social feature is any additional app functionality that enables users to share freeform content" + "or communicate with large groups of people."
- **FACT:** "Any app that includes children in their target audience and has social features, must provide an in-app reminder" + "to be safe online and to be aware of the real world risk of online interaction before allowing child users" + "to exchange freeform media or information."
- **FACT:** "You must also provide a method for adults to manage social features for child users, including, but not limited to," + "enabling/disabling the social feature or selecting different levels of functionality."
- **FACT:** "Finally, you must require adult action before enabling features that allow children to exchange personal information."
- **FACT:** "Adult action means a mechanism to verify that the user is not a child" + "and does not encourage children to falsify their age to gain access to areas of your app that are designed for adults" + "(that is, an adult PIN, password, birthdate, email verification, photo ID, credit card, or SSN)."
- **FACT:** "Social apps where the main focus of the app is to chat with people they do not know" + "or to chat with people anonymously must not target children." From August 26, 2026, anonymous chat apps may not target children at all (Preview page).
- **FACT / not found:** I did not fetch the general UGC policy page, so its moderation requirements are not verified here.
  - From memory, unverified: Play's UGC policy requires terms of use, content moderation, and an in-app way to report and block objectionable UGC and users.

### Implications for Plants vs Animals (INTERPRETATION)
- A ghost 1v1 that replays only another player's unit placements, shows no free text, and shows no player-chosen name is plausibly not a "social feature". No freeform content is shared and there is no communication.
- Showing another player's chosen name turns that name into user-generated content. That is likely to trigger the UGC policy's moderation and reporting duties, and possibly the Families social-feature duties (safety reminder and parental controls). Both conflict with the "no moderation" goal.
- Recommendation:
  - Show ghosts with auto-generated or anonymous labels ("Rival #1234") or a fixed name list, and allow no free text. Opponent pools could also be curated or developer-made.
  - Any server that stores player recordings is data collection and must go in the Data safety form. An installation-scoped ID is a device or other ID.
  - Verify the UGC policy page before building.

## 5. 16 KB memory page size

- **FACT** (https://developer.android.com/guide/practices/page-sizes): "all apps targeting Android 15 (API level 35) and higher must support 16 KB memory page sizes on 64-bit devices on Google Play. Starting February 1, 2027, if your app updates don't support 16 KB memory page sizes, you won't be able to release these updates."
- **FACT** (original announcement, https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/05/prepare-play-apps-for-devices-with-16kb-page-size.html): "Starting November 1st, 2025, all new apps and updates to existing apps submitted to Google Play and targeting Android 15+" (must support 16 KB).
- **UNCERTAIN:** the two dates differ. The current developer.android.com page gives February 1, 2027 as the hard block for updates. The blog gave November 1, 2025 as the start, and I did not find the Play Console help article explaining the transition. Treat 16 KB as already required, with hard enforcement on updates from February 1, 2027 at the latest.
- **FACT:** Native code is the scope. If your app uses any NDK libraries "either directly or indirectly through an SDK, then you will need to rebuild". Apps using only Java/Kotlin code "already supports 16 KB devices."
- **FACT** (https://developer.android.com/games/engines/unity/unity-on-android): "Unity has 16 KB page support for Unity 2021, 2022 and Unity 6."

### Implications for Plants vs Animals (INTERPRETATION)
- Unity 6000.3 supports 16 KB.
- The AdMob, Firebase and UMP native .so libraries must also be 16 KB aligned. Check the Play Console "App bundle explorer" 16 KB indicator, or run `zipalign -c -P 16`, on the 1.2.0 AAB.

Source: https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/deprecation-faq (page "last updated 2026-09-09").
- **FACT (table):**

  | Version | New app and update deadline | Extension deadline |
  |---|---|---|
  | 7 | August 31, 2026 | November 1, 2026 |
  | 8 | August 31, 2027 | November 1, 2027 |
  | 9 | August 31, 2028 | November 1, 2028 |

- **FACT (banner on the same page, conflicting):** "By Aug 31, 2026, all new apps and updates to existing apps must use Billing Library version 8 or later. If you need more time to update your app, you can request an extension until Nov 1, 2026."
- **UNCERTAIN:** the table says v7 for 2026 and the banner says v8.
- **FACT** (Payments policy, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en): apps accepting payment for in-app features "must use Google Play's billing system for those transactions unless Section 3, 8, or 9 applies."

### Implications for Plants vs Animals (INTERPRETATION)
- If IAP is added, use PBL 8 or later. The Unity IAP package version must bundle PBL 8 or later. That satisfies both readings and stays valid until August 31, 2027.
- Families rule: clearly distinguish virtual coins from real money.
- A "Remove ads" or cosmetic purchase is simple and fits the Families rules. Avoid purchase prompts during play, which risk "overly aggressive commercial tactics".

## 7. Data safety declarations for Firebase Analytics, Crashlytics and AdMob

- **FACT:** "All developers that have an app published on Google Play must complete the Data safety form." Also: "You must reflect data collection or sharing carried out by such third-party code in the Data safety form." Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469?hl=en.
- **FACT:** "'Collect' means transmitting data from your app off a user's device."

### AdMob (Google Mobile Ads SDK)
Source: https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure. The page currently describes "Google Mobile Ads SDK (Legacy) version 25.5.0".
- **FACT:** The SDK automatically collects and shares IP address, user product interactions ("app launches, taps... video views"), diagnostic information, and device or other IDs ("Android advertising (ad) ID", app set ID).
- **FACT:** Data is "encrypted in transit using the Transport Layer Security (TLS) protocol."
- **FACT:** Ad ID collection can be prevented by "updating the app's manifest file."

### Firebase Crashlytics
Source: https://firebase.google.com/docs/android/play-data-disclosure
- **FACT:** It "Collects stack traces when an application crashes", app state, and device metadata.
- **FACT:** It "Generates and stores the Crashlytics installation UUID". Custom keys and logs are collected only if you attach them.
- **FACT:** Purpose: "to enable crash reporting and crash management services." Data is encrypted in transit via HTTPS.
- **FACT** (https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy): stack traces etc. are kept "for 90 days".

### Google Analytics for Firebase
Source: https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/11582702?hl=en. The Firebase page defers to this one.
- **FACT:** It collects the app-instance ID, screen views and sessions, coarse location derived from IP, and in-app purchase events.
- **FACT:** It collects the advertising ID, but "Collection of this identifier can be disabled by configuring Analytics data collection." Data is encrypted via TLS.

### Implications for Plants vs Animals (INTERPRETATION)
- For 1.2.0, declare at least the following:
  - Location: approximate location (from IP; AdMob and Analytics).
  - App activity: app interactions (AdMob and Analytics).
  - App info and performance: crash logs and diagnostics (Crashlytics and AdMob).
  - Device or other IDs: app set ID, Analytics app-instance ID and Crashlytics installation UUID, even though AAID is off.
- Answers for those data types:
  - Collected: yes.
  - Shared: yes for AdMob (advertising).
  - Encrypted in transit: yes.
  - Purposes: Advertising, Analytics, App functionality/crash reporting, Fraud prevention.
- Version 1.1.0 ("no data collected") must not be reused as-is for 1.2.0.
- The AdMob page lists the ad ID as collected by default. Because the AD_ID permission is removed and child treatment is used, AAID is not transmitted. **ASSUMPTION:** you can then omit the advertising ID itself, but still declare the device or other IDs listed above.
- Crashlytics "free-text user IDs" are only collected if you set them, so do not set them.

## 8. Rewarded ads in Play's Ads policy

Sources: Ads policy, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en, and Better Ads Experiences, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12271244?hl=en
- **FACT:** The interstitial rule "does not apply to rewarded ads which are explicitly opted-in by users". Google's example is an ad "that developers explicitly offer a user to watch in exchange for unlocking a specific game feature."
- **FACT:** "Since users have explicitly opted in to rewarded ad experience, these ads are allowed".
- **FACT:** Full-screen interstitials "that show unexpectedly, typically when the user has chosen to do something else, are not allowed." Also: "Ads that appear during game play at the beginning of a level or during the beginning of a content segment are not allowed." Also: "Full screen interstitial ads of all formats that are not closeable after 15 seconds are not allowed."
- **FACT:** "Ads must not simulate or impersonate the user interface of any app feature." Also: "Your app cannot force a user to click an ad."
- **FACT:** "If you're monetizing an app that targets children on Google Play, it's important that your app follows the Families Ads and Monetization Policy Requirements". The stricter Families rule applies: closeable after 5 s (see Q1).

### Implications for Plants vs Animals (INTERPRETATION)
- Rewarded ads must be offered behind a clear opt-in button ("Watch ad for +50 sun?"). The button must be visually distinct from game UI, and declining must cost nothing.
- Never auto-play an ad at level start or app launch.

## 9. Challenges, time-limited events and random rewards

- **FACT** (Payments policy, answer/9858738): "Apps and games offering mechanisms to receive randomized virtual items from a purchase" + "must clearly disclose the odds of receiving those items in advance of, and in close and timely proximity to, that purchase."
- **FACT** (Real-Money Gambling, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032): "App must not provide simulated gambling content (for example, social casino apps; apps with virtual slot machines);". For gambling apps: "App must not be enrolled in the Designed for Families program;"
- **FACT:** Families rules relevant here: "Use of shocking or emotionally manipulative tactics to encourage ads viewing or in-app purchases." is not allowed, and neither is "Not providing a distinction between the use of virtual game coins versus real-life money".
- **FACT / not found:** I found no Play policy that specifically governs offline challenges, level modifiers or time-limited events.

### Implications for Plants vs Animals (INTERPRETATION)
- Offline challenges or level modifiers have no specific policy requirement.
- Random rewards that are not purchased (free drops) need no odds disclosure under the quoted rule. If any randomized item is obtainable via purchase, disclose the odds.
- For a children-included game, avoid paid randomness entirely. It is also regulated in some countries (Belgium, Netherlands); not researched here.
- Avoid countdown pressure tied to purchases or ads ("emotionally manipulative").

## 10. Account deletion when the app has no account creation

Sources: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en and the User Data policy, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en
- **FACT:** "If your app allows users to create an account from within your app, then it must also allow users" ... "to request for their account to be deleted."
- **FACT:** "Users must have a readily discoverable option to initiate app account deletion from within your app and outside of your app"
- **FACT:** An app account is defined as "A unique user identity that developers provide as a user-facing feature".
- **FACT:** All developers must answer the Data safety deletion questions, for example "Whether or not you provide a way for users to request that their data is deleted."
- **FACT:** Neither page explicitly addresses accounts created through third-party sign-in such as Google or Play Games.

### Implications for Plants vs Animals (INTERPRETATION)
- With no in-app account creation, the account deletion requirement does not apply. You still answer the deletion questions in Data safety.
- **ASSUMPTION:** PGS sign-in is a Google account, not a developer-provided account, so it probably does not trigger the requirement. PGS also provides its own deletion path (Q2 FACT). This is not explicitly confirmed by Google.
- A custom ghost/leaderboard backend with player IDs or names would likely count as an "app account". That would require in-app plus web deletion, which adds support burden.
- Cloud save via PGS Saved Games keeps deletion on Google's side. A custom cloud save would not.
